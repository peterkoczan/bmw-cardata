Your BMW's telemetry, on your own machine

BMW's CarData gives you a live MQTT stream of what your car reports. This subscribes to it, writes everything to disk, puts it in PostgreSQL, and draws a map you can scrub back through. No third party in the middle.

Source on GitHub Live demo Linking your car

What it does

  • Subscribes to the CarData MQTT stream over TLS and captures every message.
  • Keeps the raw JSONL as the source of truth, and loads it into PostgreSQL.
  • Draws your drives on a map, coloured by whether you were on battery, on petrol, or recovering energy.
  • Splits a day's fixes into separate trips, with distance, duration and consumption for each.
  • Updates live while you drive — the page follows the car without a refresh.
  • Runs in the background with a macOS menu bar icon that says whether it is actually working, not merely running.

What you need

  • A BMW with CarData, and a My BMW account where you are the primary user. CarData streaming is EU-only at present.
  • macOS for the menu bar app and launchd agents. The core — auth, the MQTT client, the database, the map — is plain Python and runs anywhere.
  • Python 3.11+ and PostgreSQL 14+. The installer sets up Postgres for you.
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/peterkoczan/bmw-cardata/main/install.sh | bash

You never give this your BMW password. You sign in on BMW's own site and approve a code — see linking your car.

How much your car tells you

Depends on the car. Mine has Live Cockpit Professional and reports its position every few minutes while driving; Live Cockpit Plus only reports at the start and end of a trip. BMW also offers far more attributes than it actually sends — of 245 streamable keys selected, about 75 have ever arrived. That is BMW's choice, not a setting.
